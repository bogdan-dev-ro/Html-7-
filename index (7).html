⚡ 2018 · The Beginning

The Gram Vision

Pavel & Nikolai Durov envision the Telegram Open Network (TON) — a blockchain fast enough, and simple enough, to serve the hundreds of millions of people already living inside Telegram.

To fund it, they raise a staggering $1.7 billion from private investors — one of the largest token pre-sales in history — built around a native currency called GRAM.

⚖️ 2020 · The Regulatory Storm

The SEC Clash

Just before GRAM's public launch, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission steps in and halts the distribution, arguing the token sale amounted to an unregistered securities offering.

After a bruising legal battle in a New York courtroom, Telegram is forced to step back — returning billions to early investors. GRAM, as a public currency, would never launch.

⛏️ 2021 · Proof-of-Work Givers

Community Resistance

The open-source spirit can't be killed. A scattered group of independent developers picks up Telegram's abandoned code and revives the network from scratch, renaming it The Open Network.

Every original allocation is locked inside Proof-of-Work Giver smart contracts — the entire supply had to be mined from zero, fairly and decentrally, by the community itself. When the dust settled, it carried a new name: Toncoin.

🚀 Present · The Unstoppable Ecosystem

The Renaissance

TON today isn't just one currency — it has grown into a full Layer-1 blockchain: a shared foundation that any project can build on and issue its own coin over.

GRAM never reached the public in 2018. Reborn by the community as Toncoin in 2021, it ran independently for years — until Telegram quietly took back control of the network's validators in May 2026. Weeks later, the community voted, and the coin came full circle: as of June 15, 2026, it's called GRAM again.
